# Healthcare Patient Data Analysis Using Microsoft Excel

## 📌 Project Overview

This project explores healthcare patient data using Microsoft Excel to identify patterns and summarize key information about patients, medical diagnoses, treatments, hospital departments, patient status, and billing.

The project demonstrates my ability to clean and validate data, perform exploratory data analysis (EDA), create PivotTables and PivotCharts, and build an interactive dashboard to communicate insights effectively.

## 🎯 Project Objectives

- Clean and validate healthcare patient data.
- Analyze patient demographics, including age and gender.
- Examine medical diagnoses and treatment types.
- Explore patient distribution across hospital departments.
- Analyze patient admission and discharge status.
- Summarize healthcare billing information.
- Analyze monthly patient admissions.
- Build an interactive Excel dashboard for data exploration.

## 🛠️ Tools and Technologies

- Microsoft Excel
- PivotTables and PivotCharts
- Excel formulas and data validation
- Slicers and interactive dashboard elements
- Data cleaning and exploratory data analysis (EDA)

## 🔍 Analysis Performed

### 1. Patient Demographics

Analyzed patient age groups and gender distribution to understand the demographic composition of the dataset.

### 2. Medical Diagnoses

Examined the distribution of recorded diagnoses to identify common medical conditions in the dataset.

### 3. Hospital Departments

Compared patient records across seven hospital departments: Cardiology, Dermatology, General Medicine, Neurology, Oncology, Orthopedics, and Pediatrics.

### 4. Treatment Analysis

Analyzed five treatment categories: Medication, Observation, Surgery, Therapy, and Vaccination.

### 5. Patient Status

Examined the distribution of patient records classified as Admitted, Discharged, or Under Treatment.

### 6. Billing Analysis

Calculated key billing statistics, including total, average, minimum, and maximum bill amounts, to understand the distribution of recorded healthcare costs.

### 7. Date Validation and Monthly Patient Admissions

Reviewed date-validation results to identify records that passed validation and records requiring further review.

Monthly patient admissions were also analyzed using admission dates to identify variations in the number of recorded admissions throughout the year.

**Key Findings:**
- **Highest admissions:** May — approximately 1,550 records.
- **Second-highest admissions:** December — approximately 1,545 records.
- **Lowest admissions:** February — approximately 1,325 records.
- **Other months:** Generally ranged between 1,400 and 1,500 records.

**Interpretation:**

Monthly patient admissions fluctuated throughout the year, with no consistent upward or downward trend. Reviewing date-validation results can also help identify records that require further checking to improve data accuracy and completeness.

*Note: Monthly admission counts show the distribution of recorded admissions, not the number of date-validation errors.*

## 💡 Key Findings

- Patients aged 60 and above represented the largest age group, accounting for **34.71%** of patient records.
- The five treatment categories had relatively similar record counts.
- Patient records were distributed across seven hospital departments.
- Admitted, discharged, and under-treatment records each represented approximately one-third of the dataset.
- Billing analysis revealed a wide range of recorded patient bill amounts.
- Monthly admission counts varied throughout the year, with May recording the highest and February the lowest.
- Date validation helped identify records requiring additional review.
